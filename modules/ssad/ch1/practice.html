Terms and notation (terminologie et notation)

↑ contents
In plain words

Read these first: every exercise below uses them. Then answer the five recall questions from memory before opening the answers.

  • Cryptography (cryptographie)designing methods to protect information, often using encryption.
  • Cryptanalysis (cryptanalyse)analysing or attacking cryptographic systems to recover information or keys without the intended secret.
  • Cryptology (cryptologie)the broader field containing both cryptography and cryptanalysis.
  • Cryptosystem (cryptosystème)the mathematical system of messages, keys, encryption functions and decryption functions.
  • Plaintext (texte clair)the original readable message.
  • Ciphertext (texte chiffré)the encrypted message.
  • Key (clé)a value controlling encryption and decryption.
  • Encryption / decryption (chiffrement / déchiffrement)plaintext → ciphertext, and ciphertext → plaintext using the appropriate key.
  • Hash (empreinte, hachage)a fixed-length fingerprint of data.
  • Collision (collision)two different inputs with the same hash.
  • Digital signature (signature numérique)a value created with a private key and verified with the matching public key.
  • Digital certificate (certificat numérique)a CA-signed document linking an identity, such as a domain, to a public key.
Notation · mathematical representation of encryption
  • P = set of plaintext (clear) messages, C = set of ciphertext messages, K = set of keys
  • E = {Ek} = family of encryption functions, D = {Dk} = family of decryption functions
  • Encryption: m ∈ P → c ∈ C, where c = Ek(m)
  • Decryption: m = Dk(c) = Dk(Ek(m))

This notation uses the same key for encryption and decryption, as in a simplified symmetric-key model. Public-key systems use a related but different key arrangement.

Quick self-check

A1

What is the difference between cryptography and cryptanalysis?

Show answer

Cryptography designs methods to protect information. Cryptanalysis studies or attacks those methods to recover information or keys without the intended secret.

A2

In c = Ek(m), what are m and c?

Show answer

m is the plaintext message; c is the ciphertext produced by encrypting m with key k.

A3

What does Dk(Ek(m)) ideally equal?

Show answer

It equals the original plaintext m, when the correct key and corresponding encryption/decryption functions are used.

A4

Why is a digital certificate not the same thing as a digital signature?

Show answer

A signature is used to sign data and verify integrity and the signing key. A certificate binds an identity to a public key and is signed by a certificate authority.

A5

If two different messages have the same hash, what is this called?

Show answer

A hash collision. Collisions must exist for fixed-size hashes, but secure cryptographic hashes are designed to make finding them infeasibly difficult.

Scenarios (mises en situation)

↑ contents
How it works

Choose the best analysis of each situation. Several options may sound related: focus on what actually happened. Picking an option marks it and shows why.

B1

Someone secretly copies a message while it travels between two colleagues. The message arrives unchanged and the service remains available. What is the main attack category?

Interception is unauthorized access to or observation of information; the message is copied rather than altered or blocked.
B2

A company server is flooded with requests and legitimate customers cannot reach the service. Which security objective is most directly affected?

Availability concerns access to systems and information when authorized users need them.
B3

An attacker edits a stored invoice so that the payee account number is different from the original. Which attack category fits best?

Modification is an unauthorized alteration of existing data.
B4

A fake IT email links to a copied sign-in page and persuades employees to enter their passwords. Which technique is central to the incident?

Phishing uses deceptive messages or pages to trick people into revealing information or taking unsafe actions.
B5

A seemingly useful utility secretly installs a hidden way for an attacker to enter the system. Which malware description best fits the disguise?

A Trojan disguises malicious behavior as legitimate or useful software.
B6

An analyst reviews timestamps and login records after an account is used at an unusual hour. Which practice is being used most directly?

Logs record events and help investigators monitor activity and detect suspicious behavior.
C

Multiple choice (QCM)

↑ contents
How it works

Recall and distinguish close concepts. Picking an option marks it and shows why.

C1

Which term means a weakness that an attacker may exploit?

A vulnerability is a weakness. A threat is a potential source of harm; a countermeasure reduces risk.
C2

Which objective checks whether a user or system is who or what it claims to be?

Authentication verifies identity or claimed origin. Access control determines what an entity is allowed to do.
C3

Which objective is primarily concerned with preventing unauthorized changes to information?

Integrity concerns the correctness of information and preventing or detecting unauthorized changes.
C4

Which statement best distinguishes passive attacks from active attacks?

Passive attacks mainly observe or collect information; active attacks change, inject, or disrupt data or activity.
C5

What does a firewall primarily do?

A firewall controls network traffic according to rules. It is one layer of defense, not a guarantee of complete security.
C6

Which description matches asymmetric cryptography?

Asymmetric cryptography uses a public/private key pair; symmetric encryption uses the same secret key for encryption and decryption.
C7

Which mechanism produces a digest for checking data integrity and is not intended to recover the original input?

Hashing produces a digest. It is not designed to be reversed to recover the input.
C8

Which malware can self-replicate across systems or networks without needing to attach itself to another host program?

A worm can self-replicate and spread across systems or networks. A classic virus attaches to a host file or program.
C9

A digital signature most directly helps provide which set of properties?

Digital signatures help verify origin and detect changes, and can support non-repudiation. They do not automatically hide content.
C10

Which example is access control rather than authentication?

Access control decides which resources or actions an entity may use. Password checking is authentication.
D

Classification (classement)

↑ contents
How it works

Choose the most specific category for each example. The verdict appears as soon as you choose.

D1

A hidden mechanism allows an attacker to bypass normal login checks.

A backdoor bypasses normal access or security controls.
D2

Malicious code activates only when a specific date or condition is reached.

A logic bomb triggers when a specified condition occurs.
D3

A program secretly monitors activity to collect information about a user.

Spyware covertly monitors activity or gathers information.
D4

Many compromised devices are remotely coordinated as one group.

A botnet is a group of compromised devices controlled or coordinated by an operator.
D5

An attacker falsifies identity information to appear to be a trusted person or device.

Spoofing falsifies identity or origin information to appear trusted.
D6

A tool monitors activity and alerts on signs of possible intrusion.

An IDS monitors events or activity and identifies potential intrusions.
D7

Malware encrypts a victim’s files and demands payment for restoring access.

Ransomware commonly blocks access to data and demands a ransom.
D8

A secret message is embedded inside an ordinary-looking image.

Steganography conceals a message inside a carrier to hide that a message exists.
E

True or false (vrai ou faux)

↑ contents
How it works

If a statement is false, try to correct it in your head before choosing.

E1

A threat and a vulnerability mean exactly the same thing.

A threat is a potential source of harm; a vulnerability is a weakness that may be exploited.
E2

Confidentiality aims to prevent unauthorized disclosure of information.

Confidentiality limits information access or disclosure to authorized parties.
E3

A digital certificate can help associate a public key with an identity.

A certificate binds identity information to a public key, typically through a trusted issuer.
E4

A firewall alone guarantees that a computer contains no malware.

A firewall controls traffic; other safeguards such as updates, safe practices, antivirus, and monitoring are also needed.
E5

Steganography and encryption are identical, and both always make a message’s existence obvious.

Encryption makes content unreadable without the key; steganography aims to conceal the existence of the message.
E6

Non-repudiation aims to make it harder for a party to credibly deny a message or action they performed.

Non-repudiation provides evidence that can help prevent denial of a transaction or message.

Authentication verifies identity; confidentiality prevents unauthorized disclosure; integrity protects against unauthorized changes; non-repudiation provides evidence against denial; availability keeps resources accessible when needed; access control restricts permitted actions and resources.

Explain the difference between hashing, encryption, and a digital signature.

Show marking guide

Hashing creates a digest for checks such as integrity; encryption protects confidentiality and can be reversed with the correct key; a digital signature helps verify origin and integrity and can support non-repudiation.

A passive attack observes or collects information, such as sniffing traffic. An active attack changes, injects, or disrupts activity, such as modifying a message or causing denial of service.
