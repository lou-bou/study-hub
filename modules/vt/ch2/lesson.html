1

What technology monitoring is (Définition de la veille technologique)

↑ contents
In plain words

A company cannot stop the world from changing: new machines, new materials and new rules appear all the time. Technology monitoring means keeping an organised watch on these changes, on purpose and on a schedule, instead of hearing about them by chance.

It is more than reading the news. The information is collected, then analysed (what does it mean for us?), then shared with the right people, who use it to decide. And it is done only by legal means.

  • technology monitoring (veille technologique)an organised, continuous watch on science and technology news that matters to a company, done to help it decide.
  • legal, lawful (licite)allowed by law. Monitoring uses public information. Stealing secrets is espionage, not monitoring.
  • systematic (de façon systématique)done with a method and on a regular schedule, not now and then when someone remembers.
  • manufacturing process (procédé de fabrication)the way a product is made: the steps, the machines, the settings.drying dates with hot air from a gas burner
  • economic impact (impact économique)what a change does to costs, prices or sales, now or later.
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

  1. Collect. Gather news on solar dryers: research articles, maker catalogues, new rules on energy use.
  2. Analyse. Ask what each item means for SahraDry: is a solar dryer cheaper over five years than gas?
  3. Share. Send a short summary to the manager, who decides.
  4. Use. The manager decides, for example to test one solar dryer next season.
  5. Legal? Reading a maker's public catalogue: yes. Asking a competitor's employee to send their supplier contract: no, that is not monitoring.

What is watched, per the course: research linked to science and technology, products, manufacturing processes, materials, and the economic impacts they have now and later. For SahraDry: drying research, dryer models, the drying method itself, panel and collector materials, and the effect on the energy bill.

What monitoring is for (Objectifs de la veille technologique)

↑ contents
In plain words

Why spend time and money watching the outside world? The course gives five reasons. In short: to make better products, to get new ideas, to keep the team sharp, to stay up to date, and above all to see changes coming before they hit you.

  • R&D (recherche et développement)research and development: the work a company does to create new products or improve old ones.
  • innovation (innovation)the ability to come up with new ideas and turn them into products or methods.
  • information flow (flux d'informations)news arriving regularly, like water in a pipe, rather than in rare bursts.
  • anticipation (anticipation)acting before a change happens, because you saw the first signs early.
  • competitive spirit (esprit concurrentiel)the wish to do better than rivals, which pushes people to learn.
Worked example

3

Monitoring questions (Exemples de questions de veille)

↑ contents
In plain words

Good monitoring starts with precise questions. "Tell me about solar energy" is too vague: you would drown in results. A precise question says exactly what you want to know, and so it also fixes where to look and where to stop.

The course groups the usual questions into four families.

  • monitoring question (question de veille)the exact question the watch must answer. It decides what is in and out of the search.
  • scope (périmètre)the limits of the search: which topic, which countries, which years.solar dryers for fruit, worldwide, last 3 years
  • patent (brevet)a public document that gives an inventor the sole right to use an invention for some years. It also describes the invention, so it is a rich source.
  • norm, standard (norme)an agreed technical rule (size, safety, quality) that products are expected to follow.
  • regulation (réglementation)the laws and official rules that apply to an activity.
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

One precise question per family for SahraDry:

  • Competition: Which date-drying companies in Algeria and Tunisia have switched to solar drying, and with which supplier?
  • Patents and rules: Which patents on solar dryers for fruit were filed since 2023? Which hygiene rules must dried dates meet to be sold in Europe?
  • New tech and materials: Which solar collector types work in a hot, dusty climate, and how much drying time do they save?
  • Research papers: Which universities have published tests of solar drying on dates?

Compare with the vague start, "solar energy": each question above names a product (dryers), a use (dates or fruit), and often a place or a period. That is what makes it searchable.

4

Who does it (Acteurs de la veille)

↑ contents
In plain words

Monitoring is team work. Three kinds of people each do one part, and each part needs the one before it.

The watchers find and gather the information. The experts work out what it means. The decision-makers use that meaning to choose what the company does.

  • watcher (veilleur)the person who searches, gathers and files information. Also called observer, researcher (recherchiste) or documentalist (documentaliste).
  • documentalist (documentaliste)a specialist in finding, sorting and storing documents.
  • raw data (données brutes)information as it was found, not yet checked or explained.
  • expert, analyst (expert, analyste)a specialist of the field who interprets the information, checks it is true (validation) and puts it in context (mise en perspective).
  • decision-maker (décideur)a manager or director who reads the summaries (synthèses de veille) and chooses the company's direction.
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

SahraDry has 12 employees, so nobody does monitoring full time.

RoleWho at SahraDryWhat they do
Watcherthe office assistant, 2 hours a weeklooks for articles, catalogues and rules on solar dryers, files them
Expertthe production engineerreads what was filed, checks it, says what it means for our drying line
Decision-makerthe managerreads the engineer's one-page summary and decides

Why not let one person do it all? The assistant does not know drying well enough to judge a technical claim, and the manager has no time to read 50 articles. Each role covers the others' gap.

5

The cycle at a glance (Processus de veille)

↑ contents
In plain words

Monitoring is done in four steps, always in the same order: decide what you need to know, find and gather the information, work out what it means, and pass it on.

It is a loop, not a line. What the managers decide usually raises a new question, which starts the next round.

  • process (processus)a fixed series of steps that turns something in (a need) into something out (a decision).
  • need (besoin)what the company wants to know. Step 1 turns it into a precise question.
  • collection, gathering (récolte, collecte)getting hold of the information from the sources found.
  • processing (traitement)sorting, checking, summarising and explaining the information.
  • diffusion, sharing (diffusion)sending the results to the people who need them, in a form they can use.
Worked example

Step 1: define the need (Définition des besoins (sur quoi veiller ?))

↑ contents
In plain words

Before searching, decide what you are looking for and why. Skipping this step is the main cause of failed monitoring: you collect a lot and answer nothing.

The course lists seven small tasks. Think of a funnel: you pour in a vague topic and get out a precise question, plus a plan to answer it.

  • keyword (mot-clé)a word or short phrase you will type into search tools."solar dryer", "dates"
  • scope (périmètre)the limits of the subject: what is in, what is out.
  • guiding idea (idée directrice)one sentence that says what the watch is for. It keeps the work on track.
  • resources (ressources)what the work needs: people, time, money, tools, access to databases.
  • plan (planifier)decide who does what, and when.
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

  1. Understand the work and its goal (comprendre la nature, les objectifs). The manager must decide within 6 months whether to invest in solar drying.
  2. Make the question precise (préciser la question de veille). Which solar dryers suit dates, what do they cost, and how much gas do they save?
  3. Pull out the key ideas and keywords (idées importantes, mots-clés). solar dryer, solar tunnel dryer, dates, dried fruit, drying time, energy cost; in French: séchoir solaire, dattes.
  4. Set the scope (périmètre). Dryers for fruit only (not grain), hot dry climates, items from the last 3 years.
  5. Write the guiding idea (idée directrice). "Find out if solar drying can cut our drying cost without hurting date quality."
  6. List the resources (ressources nécessaires). The assistant 2 hours a week, the engineer 1 hour a week, free tools, a library access for papers.
  7. Plan (planifier). Months 1 to 2 collect, month 3 analyse, month 4 report to the manager.
7

Step 2a: find the sources and judge them (Identification des sources et sourcing)

↑ contents
In plain words

Once you know the question, you need places to look: these are the sources. Finding good sources is a job in itself, called sourcing, and it needs a human: a person must judge whether a source is worth watching.

After that, software monitoring agents can keep an eye on the chosen sources automatically. But they only watch what a human picked first.

To judge a source, the course gives four checks: who is behind it, how recent it is, whether it is neutral, and whether it is well organised.

  • source (source d'information)any place information comes from: a website, a newspaper, a database, a forum...
  • sourcing (sourcing)the work of finding and choosing the sources worth watching. Done by the watchers.
  • monitoring agent (agent de veille)a program that checks chosen sources regularly and reports what is new.
  • institutional site (site institutionnel)the website of an official body: ministry, university, research centre, standards office.
  • thematic directory (annuaire thématique)a list of websites sorted by subject by people (not by a robot).
  • database (base de données)an organised, searchable collection of records, such as scientific articles or patents.
  • authority (autorité)how much we can trust the author or publisher: known, qualified, accountable?
  • objectivity (objectivité)being neutral: not trying to sell you something or push one side.

Step 2b: collect, by pull or by push (Collecte d'information : modes Pull et Push)

↑ contents
In plain words

There are two ways to get information from the web.

Pull: you go and get it yourself, like going fishing. You type searches, open pages, keep what is good. Push: the information comes to you on its own. You set up an alert once (keywords, filters), and a tool sends you new items as they appear.

Pull is precise but slow. Push saves time but, if your keywords are too broad, it buries you under results.

  • pull mode (mode Pull)you search the web yourself, each time you need something.
  • push mode (mode Push)a tool sends you new information automatically, based on settings you chose once.
  • alert (alerte)an automatic message (often an email) saying a new item matches your keywords.
  • settings, configuration (paramétrage)choosing the keywords, filters and sources an alert uses. Poor settings bring floods of useless items.
  • registration (enregistrement)creating an account with a publisher or database. Pull needs none; many push services do.
  • spam (spam)unwanted mass messages that clog your inbox.
Worked example
The example, in full

Step 2c: the right tool, and storing what you collect (Collecte d'information : outils et stockage)

↑ contents
In plain words

Watchers must master their tools. Before picking one, ask three questions: what kind of tool is it, how does it work, and what must I set up? Then match it to the source and to the purpose.

The course names three purposes: monitor a page for changes, copy a whole site, or detect new items. Finally, what you collect must be checked, sorted and saved somewhere suitable, so step 3 can use it. Monitoring software helps with that.

  • monitor a page (surveiller)the tool revisits a page and tells you when it changes.a ministry's page of new energy subsidies
  • copy a site (aspirer un site)download a whole website to your computer to read or search it offline ("vacuum" it).HTTrack
  • detect (détecter)scan many sources for new items that match keywords.
  • validate (valider)check an item is true and relevant before keeping it.
  • record, file (consigner)write it down in a set place and format (a database, a shared folder) with its source and date.
  • medium (support)where the information is kept: a file, a database, a notes tool...
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

Step 3: analyse and process (Analyse et traitement de l'information)

↑ contents
In plain words

This is where information gains value. A pile of articles is not useful to a manager. What is useful is: "here is what it means for us, and here is what we could do".

The collected items are checked, analysed and summarised to link them together, make hypotheses, sketch scenarios and make proposals. It is the hardest and most important step, the heart of the process, so it is usually done by experts of the field, helped by software.

  • added value (valeur ajoutée)what the analysis adds on top of the raw items: meaning, links, advice.
  • hypothesis (hypothèse)a guess you can test."if we keep gas, our cost will pass our rival's"
  • scenario (scénario (pl. scénarii))a possible future, described with its consequences.
  • classification (classification)sorting items into groups.
  • mapping (cartographie)drawing a map of a field: who works with whom, which topics are linked.
  • text mining (text mining (fouille de textes))software that reads many texts and pulls out frequent words, topics and trends.
  • sense-making (création de sens)turning scattered facts into a clear story you can act on.
Worked example
The example, in full

SahraDry is a small company in Biskra (12 employees) that dries dates for export. Its dryers run on gas, and the gas bill keeps rising. The manager wants to know: could solar dryers replace gas?

Four items were collected and checked this month (invented for the example): (a) a study says a solar tunnel dryer dries dates 30% faster than open-air drying; (b) the industrial gas price will rise next year; (c) a competitor in Tolga switched to solar drying; (d) a supplier offers solar dryers on lease.

  1. Links. (b) + (c): our gas bill rises while the competitor's does not, so its drying cost will drop below ours.
  2. Hypothesis. "If we stay on gas, within two years our cost per tonne will be higher than the competitor's."
  3. Scenarios. A: keep gas and accept higher costs. B: lease one solar dryer for one season and measure. C: buy a full solar line now.
  4. Proposal. Scenario B: low risk thanks to (d), real data from our own site, and a decision on C after one season.

Notice what changed: the manager no longer gets four articles, but one proposal with its reasons. That is the added value.

Tools for handling documents (Outils d'analyse de données et de gestion des documents)

↑ contents
In plain words

Analysis needs order. The course names three families of tools to keep what you collected tidy, annotated and shareable. All three have a browser extension, so you can save something with one click while reading.

  • reference (référence)the details needed to find a document again: author, title, date, link.
  • reference manager (outil de gestion bibliographique)software that stores references and their PDFs, and writes bibliographies for you. Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote.
  • annotate (annoter)add your own notes, highlights or comments onto a document.
  • social bookmarking (social bookmarking)saving web pages (bookmarks) online, with notes and tags, and sharing them with others. Diigo, Pearltrees.
  • tag (tag, étiquette)a short label you attach to an item so you can find all items on one theme.#tunnel-dryer
  • browser extension (extension de navigateur)a small add-on to Chrome or Firefox that adds a button, here "save this page".
  • collaborative work (travail collaboratif)several people working on the same shared folders at the same time.

Step 4: share the results (Diffusion de l'information)

↑ contents
In plain words

The work only pays off if the right people get it. Diffusion means sending the results, regularly, in the form that suits each reader.

Three questions decide it: who must be told (their profile), how (paper, email, intranet, portal), and when (how often). And it must be selective: each person gets only what concerns them. Sending everything to everyone drowns people, and the important item gets lost.

  • user profile (profil d'utilisateur)what a reader needs to know and in what form, given their job.
  • frequency (fréquence)how often results are sent: at once, weekly, monthly...
  • intranet (intranet)a private website only the company's staff can open.
  • monitoring portal (portail de veille)a website (often on the intranet) where all monitoring results are published and searchable.
  • email push (e-mail « push »)results sent straight into people's inboxes, without them asking each time.
  • selective diffusion (diffusion sélective de l'information (DSI))sending each person only the items that match their profile.
Worked example
The example, in full

What monitoring delivers, and tools to share it (Produits, livrables et outils de partage)

↑ contents
In plain words

The results of monitoring take the shape of products (also called deliverables): a newsletter, a file on one theme, a profile of a sector, figures, alerts... Each suits a different reader and rhythm.

Many free web tools help share them: notes, bibliographies, slides, idea maps, curation pages, dashboards, blogs.

  • deliverable (livrable)a finished product handed to a reader.
  • newsletter, monitoring bulletin (bulletin de veille)a short regular digest of the latest news on a topic.
  • thematic dossier (dossier thématique)a full file on one subject, with analysis.
  • sector profile (profil sectoriel)a portrait of an industry: main firms, figures, trends.
  • curation (curation)picking the best items on a topic and republishing them with a short comment.Scoop.it
  • dashboard (tableau de bord)one screen that gathers several feeds or indicators.Netvibes
  • concept map (carte conceptuelle)a diagram of ideas connected by arrows.Mindmeister

A panorama of monitoring tools (Panorama des outils de la veille)

↑ contents
In plain words

There are a great many monitoring tools. The course sorts them in two ways.

By age: classic tools for searching the internet (search engines, directories, meta-search engines) and newer tools (monitoring agents, analysis and formatting tools, full monitoring software suites). By coverage: you can use a different tool for each step, or one suite that covers all four steps.

  • search engine (moteur de recherche)a site that searches the web for your words.Google
  • directory (annuaire)a list of websites sorted by subject by people.
  • meta-search engine (méta-moteur)a tool that sends your search to several search engines at once and merges their results.
  • monitoring agent (agent de surveillance)a program that watches chosen sources and reports changes.
  • software suite, package (progiciel)ready-made professional software that does a whole job, here every step of monitoring.
  • web clipping (web clipping)cutting out and saving a piece of a web page (text, image) to keep it.
  • streaming (streaming)sending video or sound live over the internet, watched as it plays.
