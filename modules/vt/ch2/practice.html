A2Which step is it?Warm-up
The cycle: 1 Define the need (définition des besoins) → 2 Find sources and collect (identification des sources, récolte) → 3 Analyse and process (analyse et traitement) → 4 Share (diffusion) → decision → back to 1.

A small company, SahraDry, dries dates and wonders whether solar dryers could replace gas. Which step of the process is each task part of?

Show solution
  • Writing down the keywords and the scope of the search: 1 · Define the need
  • Checking whether a website's author is qualified: 2 · Sources and collection
  • Setting up an email alert on "solar dryer": 2 · Sources and collection
  • Linking a gas price rise to a competitor's switch to solar: 3 · Analysis
  • Writing two possible scenarios and a proposal: 3 · Analysis
  • Deciding who receives which items, and how often: 4 · Diffusion
  • Sending a one-page monthly bulletin to the manager: 4 · Diffusion

Tip: anything about where to look or getting items is step 2; anything about what it means is step 3; anything about who gets it, how, when is step 4.

A3Who does it?Standard
Roles: watchers = information professionals (observers, researchers, documentalists); experts = domain specialists (analysts, technical experts); decision-makers = managers, directors.

Monitoring is team work with three roles. Who does each task?

Show solution
  • Collects raw data and manages the sources: watcher (veilleur)
  • Interprets and validates the collected information: expert / analyst
  • Uses the monitoring summaries to choose the company's direction: decision-maker (décideur)
  • Searches for relevant sources (sourcing): watcher (veilleur)
  • Puts the information in perspective for the company: expert / analyst
  • Decides to test one solar dryer next season: decision-maker (décideur)

B1Pull or push?Warm-up
Pull: you search the web yourself. Push: a tool sends you new items automatically, from settings (keywords, filters) you chose once.

Pull or push?

Show solution
  • You go fishing for the information yourself: pull
  • No registration with a publisher or database is needed: pull
  • Risk of being flooded if the keywords are too broad: push
  • Automatic, warns you quickly: push
  • Takes a lot of time, is tedious, and the watch becomes irregular: pull
  • You can discard bad results at once, so it is precise: pull
  • Saves the researcher time and keeps the watch regular: push
  • Brings emails and spam: push
B2Which check does it fail?Standard
Four checks: authority (who is behind it?), date (are the pages you use recent? check them all, not just the home page), objectivity (is it neutral?), organisation (can you find things in it?).

Each source fails one of the four quality checks. Which one?

Show solution
  • A report on solar dryers written by a dryer seller, praising its own model: objectivity (objectivité)
  • A site whose home page is dated today, but whose technical pages were last updated in 2015: date of update (mise à jour)
  • An article with no author name and no known publisher: authority (autorité)
  • Useful data scattered over pages with no menu and broken links: organisation of the site
B3The right tool for the jobStandard

Pick the tool family that fits each job.

Show solution
  • Store 20 scientific PDFs and cite them in a Word report: reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley)
  • Capture a maker's web page and highlight the price on it: social bookmarking (Diigo, Pearltrees)
  • Download a whole research centre's website to read it offline: site copier (HTTrack)
  • Republish the 10 best articles of the month, each with a comment: curation (Scoop.it)
  • Gather several news feeds on a single screen: dashboard (Netvibes)
  • Write notes after a supplier call and share them with a colleague: note tool (Evernote, Google Keep)

D1True or false?Standard
Technology monitoring is limited to collecting information on the internet.

Show solution

False. Collection is only step 2. Monitoring also includes analysis, summarising and sharing useful information for strategic decisions.

D2True or false?Standard
Sourcing can be fully automated with digital tools.

Show solution

False. Tools can automate the searching, but choosing the relevant sources needs human judgement to make sure they are reliable and relevant.

D3True or false?Standard
Push mode delivers the most relevant information automatically, with no set-up needed.

Show solution

False. Push needs precise settings (keywords, filters, alerts); otherwise it floods you with irrelevant items.

D4True or false?Standard
Analysis and processing is the heart of the monitoring process.

Show solution

True. This is the step where collected data becomes useful information, interpreted and put in perspective for decisions.

D5True or false?Standard
Effective monitoring needs several profiles with complementary skills.

Show solution

True. Watchers collect, analysts interpret, decision-makers act: each turns data one step closer to a usable strategy.

D6True or false?Standard
Selective diffusion aims to avoid overloading the people who receive it.

Show solution

True. Too much information wastes time and buries decision-makers in useless data. Selecting makes the watch more effective.

E4Three questions to think aboutChallenge

The course's reflection questions. Answer each in two or three sentences.

Show solution
  1. Each role is complementary: watchers collect, analysts interpret, decision-makers use the information to act. Remove one and the chain breaks (no data, no meaning, or no action).
  2. Combine automation with human control, and judge sources with clear criteria: author (authority), the source itself, and the date of last update.
  3. For one-off, precise searches: you pick only relevant results, so you avoid the overload a push alert can bring.
