A1Which type of monitoring catches the signal?Warm-up

Each signal comes in from the outside. Which type of monitoring (veille) is responsible for it?

Types: competitive (rivals) · commercial (clients, suppliers, distributors) · marketing (market, consumers, campaigns) · technological (standards, patents, processes) · legal (laws, case law, counterfeits) · e-reputation (brand image online)
1. A rival chain changes its pricing policy and opens new stores
2. Your main distributor signs an exclusive contract with another brand
3. A survey shows young customers now buy mostly through Instagram
4. A new standard for IoT device security is published
5. A court ruling changes how online contracts must be signed
6. Customers post angry reviews about late deliveries on social networks
7. Fake copies of your product appear on a marketplace
8. A university lab publishes a faster image-compression process
Show solution
  1. A rival chain changes its pricing policy and opens new stores: competitive (prices, store network of a competitor)
  2. Your main distributor signs an exclusive contract with another brand: commercial (distributor = trading partner)
  3. A survey shows young customers now buy mostly through Instagram: marketing (consumer behaviour)
  4. A new standard for IoT device security is published: technological (standards = normes)
  5. A court ruling changes how online contracts must be signed: legal (case law = jurisprudence)
  6. Customers post angry reviews about late deliveries on social networks: e-reputation (online image)
  7. Fake copies of your product appear on a marketplace: legal (counterfeits = contrefaçons)
  8. A university lab publishes a faster image-compression process: technological (new process)
A2Which SWOT box?Warm-up

A small Algerian online bookshop lists what it knows. Put each item in its box.

Rule: inside the company and controllable → S (helps) or W (hurts). Outside, not controllable → O (helps) or T (hurts).
1. Its own fast delivery network, built over 5 years
2. Its website looks outdated and loads slowly
3. More and more customers start paying online
4. A large international marketplace starts delivering locally
5. A loyal customer base among students
6. Only one person handles all the marketing
7. A new university opens in the same city
8. Paper prices rise sharply worldwide
Show solution
  1. Its own fast delivery network, built over 5 years: S · Strength (internal, helps)
  2. Its website looks outdated and loads slowly: W · Weakness (internal, hurts; the shop can fix it)
  3. More and more customers start paying online: O · Opportunity (external trend that helps)
  4. A large international marketplace starts delivering locally: T · Threat (external competitor)
  5. A loyal customer base among students: S · Strength (internal asset)
  6. Only one person handles all the marketing: W · Weakness (internal, controllable)
  7. A new university opens in the same city: O · Opportunity (external, new customers)
  8. Paper prices rise sharply worldwide: T · Threat (external, beyond control)

C1About monitoringStandard

True or false? Then justify the false ones in your own words.

1. Competitive monitoring only looks at direct competitors.
2. A company that does no technology monitoring can stay competitive in the long run.
3. The goal of monitoring is to eliminate all risk.
4. Monitoring is a one-off activity answering a precise need.
5. Strategic monitoring brings the other types of monitoring together.

Show solution
  1. False: it also covers indirect, current and potential competitors.
  2. False: it risks missing major technological shifts (think Kodak).
  3. False: it identifies risks so they can be managed proactively; it does not remove them.
  4. False: that is information search; monitoring is continuous.
  5. True: it federates them and adds the strategic, action-oriented dimension.
C2About SWOTStandard

True or false? Then justify the false ones in your own words.

1. SWOT analysis is only used in marketing.
2. Opportunities and threats are always controllable by the company.
3. A badly exploited opportunity can turn into a threat.
4. Weaknesses are controllable and should be minimised.
5. Two people doing the same SWOT will almost always get the same result.

Show solution
  1. False: it is used in strategy, monitoring, project management, even personal planning.
  2. False: they come from the external environment (market, technology, law).
  3. True: if you do not act on a new technology, competitors use it against you.
  4. True: they are internal, so the company can work on them.
  5. False: SWOT is subjective; use it as a guide, not a prescription.

D1Commercial vs marketingStandard

Distinguish commercial monitoring from marketing monitoring, and give one example of each.

Show solution

Commercial is operational: day-to-day relations of buying and selling with clients, suppliers and distributors. Example: noticing that a supplier lowers its prices.

Marketing is strategic: the market and consumer behaviour, to shape the offer and the communication. Example: spotting the organic trend and adapting the range.

They are complementary: marketing prepares the long-term strategy, commercial helps manage business relations day to day.

D3A monitoring planChallenge

A start-up wants to launch a mobile payment app in Algeria. Which types of monitoring should it set up, and what would each one watch?

Show solution
  • Technological: payment security standards, new payment technologies (QR, NFC), patents.
  • Legal: banking and e-payment regulation, personal data protection rules.
  • Competitive: existing payment apps and banks' own apps, their fees and features.
  • Commercial: merchants who could accept the app, partner banks, telecom operators.
  • Marketing: how people pay today, their trust in online payment.
  • E-reputation: user reviews and social media talk about the app.

Strategic monitoring combines all of this to decide when and how to launch.

D4Making SWOT predictiveChallenge

Explain how strategic monitoring can turn a classic SWOT into a tool for anticipating. Use Kodak or another example.

Show solution

A classic SWOT is a picture of the situation at one moment. Strategic monitoring adds a time dimension: it keeps detecting early signals (patents, trends, new regulations) and updates the O and T boxes before the changes become critical.

Kodak: an up-to-date SWOT would have listed digital photography as a growing threat to film and an opportunity for an "imaging" company. Acting on it would have meant repositioning before the film market collapsed.
