1

Standard form (forme standard)

↑ contents
In plain words

The simplex works with equations, not inequalities. So each constraint ≤ gets its own extra variable, a slack, that measures how much of the resource is left unused. A slack is never negative, and it is zero exactly when the constraint is tight.

For a constraint ≥, you subtract a variable instead: a₁x₁ + … + aₙxₙ − e₁ = b₁ with e₁ ≥ 0.

  • linear program (programme linéaire)maximise a linear function of the variables subject to linear constraints and x ≥ 0.
  • objective function (fonction économique)the quantity to maximise, here the profit Z.Z = 7x₁ + 5x₂
  • feasible region (domaine des solutions réalisables)all points that satisfy every constraint; for two variables a polygon.
  • slack variable (variable d'écart)added to a ≤ constraint to make it an equation; the unused capacity.x₁ + x₃ = 300, x₃ ≥ 0
  • standard form (programme linéaire standard)every constraint an equation, every variable ≥ 0.
Worked example · Example 8.4.3, the cabinet-maker (l'ébéniste)

A cabinet-maker makes luxury desks (x1) and standard desks (x2). Sales are capped at 300 luxury and 400 standard desks; wood is enough for 500 desks of any kind; a luxury desk takes twice the time of a standard one, and time is enough for 700 standard desks. Margins are 7 and 5.

  • Program: x1 ≤ 300, x2 ≤ 400, x1 + x2 ≤ 500, 2x1 + x2 ≤ 700, x1, x2 ≥ 0; maximise Z = 7x1 + 5x2.
  • Standard form: one slack per constraint: x1 + x3 = 300, x2 + x4 = 400, x1 + x2 + x5 = 500, 2x1 + x2 + x6 = 700, all six variables ≥ 0.
  • Reading slacks at P(100, 200): x3 = 300 − 100 = 200 luxury sales left, x4 = 400 − 200 = 200, x5 = 500 − 300 = 200 desks of wood left, x6 = 700 − 400 = 300 units of time left.
2

Basic solutions and the first tableau (solutions de base, tableau initial)

↑ contents
In plain words

With 2 real variables and 4 slacks there are 6 unknowns but only 4 equations. Set 2 variables to zero and the other 4 follow. If they all come out ≥ 0, you are standing on a corner of the region: that is a basic solution.

The zero variables are out of the basis, the others are in the basis. The simplex starts at the origin, where the slacks form the basis.

  • basic variable (variable dans la base, VDB)a variable read off the tableau; usually non-zero.at O: x₃, x₄, x₅, x₆
  • non-basic variable (variable hors-base, VHB)a variable set to zero. With m constraints and n real variables there are at least n of them and at most m basic ones.at O: x₁, x₂
  • basic solution (solution de base)a feasible solution with that split; geometrically a vertex of the region.
  • simplex tableau (tableau du simplexe)one row per basic variable, one for Z; one column per variable, plus the constants (cste).
Worked example · first tableau of Example 8.4.3

Start by producing nothing: x1 = x2 = 0 (out of the basis), so x3 = 300, x4 = 400, x5 = 500, x6 = 700 (in the basis) and Z = 0.

3

Who enters, who leaves (variable entrante, variable sortante)

↑ contents
In plain words

From a corner, raise one zero variable. Pick the one whose coefficient in the Z row is the largest positive: each unit of it adds the most profit. That variable enters.

Raise it until the first basic variable hits zero; any further and that variable would turn negative. That one leaves. To find it, divide each cste by the entering column and take the smallest ratio.

  • entering variable (variable entrante)the non-basic variable that becomes positive.
  • Dantzig's rule (critère de Dantzig, règle du plus grand gain marginal)choose the largest strictly positive coefficient of the Z row (Z written with non-basic variables only).Z = 7x₁ + 5x₂ → x₁
  • ratio column (colonne C)cste ÷ coefficient of the entering column, for rows whose coefficient is > 0.
  • leaving variable (variable sortante)the basic variable of the row with the smallest ratio.
  • pivot (pivot)the coefficient at the crossing of the entering column and the leaving row.
Worked example · first iteration of Example 8.4.3
  • Enter: Z = 7x1 + 5x2; 7 is the largest positive coefficient, so x1 enters.
  • Ratios: x3: 300 ÷ 1 = 300; x4: +∞ (coefficient 0); x5: 500 ÷ 1 = 500; x6: 700 ÷ 2 = 350.
  • Leave: the smallest ratio is 300, in the x3 row, so x3 leaves. Pivot = 1.
  • Check by hand: with x2 = 0, x3 = 300 − x1 ≥ 0, x5 = 500 − x1 ≥ 0, x6 = 700 − 2x1 ≥ 0 give x1 ≤ 300, 500, 350; the tightest is 300.
Only rows with a positive coefficient count. A coefficient 0 means the row never limits the entering variable (ratio +∞). A negative coefficient means that basic variable grows, so it cannot leave; skip that row even though cste ÷ coefficient is a number (−50 in the third tableau below).
4

Pivoting the tableau (le pivotage)

↑ contents
In plain words

Once you know who enters and who leaves, rewrite the whole system in terms of the new non-basic variables. In the tableau that is two moves: divide the pivot row by the pivot, then subtract the right multiple of it from every other row (Z row included) so the entering column becomes 0 everywhere except a 1 on the pivot row.

In the basis column (VDB), the leaving variable is replaced by the entering one, in the same place.

  • pivot row (ligne pivot, Lp)the leaving variable's row, divided by the pivot.
  • row operation (combinaison linéaire de lignes)Li − (coefficient of the entering variable in Li) × Lp.Lx₆ − 2Lp
  • iteration (itération)one pivot: one move from a vertex to an adjacent vertex.
Worked example · the full run of Example 8.4.3
  1. Iteration 1: x1 enters (coefficient 7), ratios 300, +∞, 500, 350, so x3 leaves; pivot 1. Row operations: Lx5 − Lp, Lx6 − 2Lp, LZ − 7Lp. New vertex (300, 0), Z = 2100.
  2. Iteration 2: x2 enters (coefficient 5), ratios +∞, 400, 200, 100, so x6 leaves; pivot 1. Row operations: Lx4 − Lp, Lx5 − Lp, LZ − 5Lp. New vertex (300, 100), Z = 2600.
  3. Iteration 3: x3 enters (coefficient 3), ratios 300, 150, 100, skip, so x5 leaves; pivot 1. Row operations: Lx1 − Lp, Lx4 − 2Lp, Lx2 + 2Lp, LZ − 3Lp. New vertex (200, 300), Z = 2900.

5

Stopping and reading the answer (critère d'arrêt, interprétation)

↑ contents
In plain words

Stop when no coefficient in the Z row is positive. Then Z is written as a constant minus non-negative multiples of the non-basic variables, so raising any of them can only lower Z: the current corner is optimal.

Read the answer from the cste column: non-basic variables are 0, each basic variable equals its cste, and Z is minus the Z-row cste.

  • optimality test (critère d'optimalité)all Z-row coefficients ≤ 0 (for a maximisation).Z = 2900 − 3x₅ − 2x₆
  • optimal solution (solution optimale)the feasible point with the largest Z.
  • level line (droite d'isoprofit, DZ)all points with the same Z; the optimum is where the last one touches the region.
Worked example · reading the last tableau of Example 8.4.3
  • Z row: 0, 0, 0, 0, −3, −2 | −2900, that is Z = 2900 − 3x5 − 2x6. No positive coefficient: stop.
  • x1 = 200 luxury desks, x2 = 300 standard desks, Z = 2900.
  • x3 = 100: 100 more luxury desks could still be sold. x4 = 100: same for standard desks.
  • x5 = 0: all the wood is used. x6 = 0: all the time is used.

6

Pivots that are not 1 (pivot différent de 1)

↑ contents
In plain words

Nothing changes in the method, but the pivot row must first be divided by the pivot so that the pivot becomes 1. Only then do you clear the rest of the column. Fractions appear; keep them exact rather than rounding.

With three real variables you can no longer draw the region, but the tableau works the same way.

  • normalised pivot row (nouvelle ligne pivot)the pivot row divided by the pivot.pivot 3: (1, 3, 2, 1, 0, 0 | 40) → (1/3, 1, 2/3, 1/3, 0, 0 | 40/3)
  • exact arithmetic (calcul en fractions)keep 55/7, not 7.86: rounding errors pile up from one tableau to the next.
Worked example · Example 8.4.4

Maximise Z = 10x1 + 14x2 + 12x3 subject to x1 + 3x2 + 2x3 ≤ 40, 3x1 + 2x2 + x3 ≤ 45, x1 + x2 + 4x3 ≤ 38, all ≥ 0. Slacks x4, x5, x6.

  1. Iteration 1: x2 enters (coefficient 14). Ratios: x4: 40 ÷ 3 = 40/3; x5: 45 ÷ 2 = 45/2; x6: 38 ÷ 1 = 38. x4 leaves, pivot 3. New point (0, 40/3, 0), Z = 560/3.
  2. Iteration 2: x1 enters (coefficient 16/3). Ratios: x2: 40/3 ÷ (1/3) = 40; x5: 55/3 ÷ (7/3) = 55/7; x6: 74/3 ÷ (2/3) = 37. x5 leaves, pivot 7/3. New point (55/7, 75/7, 0), Z = 1600/7.
  3. Iteration 3: x3 enters (coefficient 24/7). Ratios: x2: 75/7 ÷ (5/7) = 15; x1: skipped (coefficient −1/7 < 0); x6: 136/7 ÷ (24/7) = 17/3. x6 leaves, pivot 24/7. New point (26/3, 20/3, 17/3), Z = 248.

7

Infinitely many optima (infinité de solutions)

↑ contents
In plain words

When the level lines of Z are parallel to an edge of the region, the whole edge is optimal. The tableau shows it too: at the optimum, a non-basic variable has coefficient 0 in the Z row. Letting it enter moves to the next vertex without changing Z.

  • alternative optimum (solution optimale multiple)another vertex with the same optimal Z.
  • zero reduced cost (coefficient nul dans la fonction économique)a 0 in the Z row under a non-basic variable at the optimum.Z = 100 − x₅ + 0·x₃
  • optimal edge (segment optimal)every point between two adjacent optimal vertices is optimal too.[BC]
Worked example · Example 8.4.5

Maximise Z = x1 + 2x2 subject to x2 ≤ 45, 3x1 + x2 ≤ 120, x1 + 2x2 ≤ 100, x1 + x2 ≤ 60. Slacks x3 to x6.

  • Iteration 1: x2 enters (2). Ratios: x3: 45 ÷ 1 = 45; x4: 120 ÷ 1 = 120; x5: 100 ÷ 2 = 50; x6: 60 ÷ 1 = 60. x3 leaves → A(0, 45), Z = 90.
  • Iteration 2: Z = x1 − 2x3 + 90, so x1 enters. Ratios: x2: +∞ (coefficient 0); x4: 75 ÷ 3 = 25; x5: 10 ÷ 1 = 10; x6: 15 ÷ 1 = 15. x5 leaves → B(10, 45), Z = 100.
  • Optimal already: Z = 100 − x5 + 0·x3. Nothing positive, but x3 has a 0.
  • Iteration 3 (optional): let x3 enter. Ratios: x2: 45 ÷ 1 = 45; x4: 45 ÷ 5 = 9; x1: skipped (coefficient −2 < 0); x6: 5 ÷ 1 = 5. x6 leaves → C(20, 40), Z = 100 again.
  • Every point of [BC]: x₁ + 2x₂ = 100 with 10 ≤ x₁ ≤ 20; e.g. the midpoint (15, 42.5) gives 15 + 85 = 100.

8

Cells you can copy (cases recopiées sans calcul)

↑ contents
In plain words

Two shortcuts save most of the arithmetic. A 0 in the pivot row means that column does not change: copy it. A 0 in the pivot column means that row does not change: copy it. The pivot row itself (divided by the pivot) and the new unit column of the entering variable are known too.

  • invariant column (colonne invariante)a column with 0 in the pivot row is unchanged by the pivot.
  • invariant row (ligne invariante)a row with 0 in the pivot column is unchanged by the pivot.
Worked example · Remark 8.4.1 on Example 8.4.3
  • First pivot (x1 in, x3 out, pivot row 1 0 1 0 0 0 | 300): the zeros under x2, x4, x5, x6 mean those four columns are copied unchanged into the second tableau.
  • Second pivot (x2 in, x6 out, pivot row 0 1 −2 0 0 1 | 100): zeros under x1, x4, x5 → copy those columns. The 0 of row x1 in the x2 column → copy row x1.
  • What is left: rows x4, x5, Z in columns x3, x6, cste: 9 cells, e.g. x4 row: (0 1 0 1 0 0 | 400) − (0 1 −2 0 0 1 | 100) = (0 0 2 1 0 −1 | 300).
9

Breaking a tie (égalité des coefficients)

↑ contents
In plain words

If two variables share the largest positive coefficient, Dantzig's rule does not decide. Do the ratio test for each candidate: the profit gained by a pivot is coefficient × ratio. With equal coefficients, take the candidate with the larger ratio.

  • gain of a pivot (accroissement de Z)coefficient of the entering variable × its smallest ratio.2 × 50 = 100
  • tie (ex æquo)two equal largest positive coefficients in the Z row.
Worked example · Example 8.4.6

Maximise Z = 2x1 + 2x2 + x3 subject to 3x1 + 5x2 + x3 ≤ 150 and x1 + 4x2 + 2x3 ≤ 80, all ≥ 0. Slacks x4, x5.

  • If x1 enters: ratios 150 ÷ 3 = 50, 80 ÷ 1 = 80 → x4 leaves, pivot 3, ratio 50, Z rises to 2 × 50 = 100.
  • If x2 enters: ratios 150 ÷ 5 = 30, 80 ÷ 4 = 20 → x5 leaves, pivot 4, ratio 20, Z rises to 2 × 20 = 40.
  • Choice: 50 > 20, so x1 enters and x4 leaves.
  • Going on (not in the course): next x3 enters (coefficient 1/3), x5 leaves, and the optimum is reached: (44, 0, 18), Z = 106, after 2 pivots. Starting with x2 also ends at Z = 106, but after 3 pivots.
10

Not the shortest path (le critère de Dantzig n'est pas le plus court chemin)

↑ contents
In plain words

Dantzig's rule looks at the gain per unit, not at how far the variable can go. It always reaches the optimum, but it can take a long detour. Example 8.4.7 is built so that it visits every one of the 8 corners.

  • greedy rule (règle gloutonne)a choice that looks best right now, with no look-ahead.
  • adjacent vertices (sommets adjacents)two corners joined by an edge; one pivot moves between them.

Maximise Z = 4x1 + 2x2 + x3 subject to x1 ≤ 5, 4x1 + x2 ≤ 25, 8x1 + 4x2 + x3 ≤ 125, all ≥ 0. Slacks x4, x5, x6.

  • Nothing in the Z row could have told you this in advance: that is why the course says the rule gives the optimum but not the shortest path.
