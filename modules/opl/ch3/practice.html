A1From words to a programWarm-up

A workshop makes chairs (x1) and tables (x2). Each piece needs 1 hour of labour, and 8 hours are available. A chair takes 2 hours on the machine and a table 1 hour; the machine runs 12 hours. At most 6 tables can be sold. Profit: 3 per chair, 2 per table.

1. Which constraint is the machine?

2. What is the objective function?

3. How many slack variables does the standard form need?

Show solution
  1. 2x1 + x2 ≤ 12: 2 machine hours per chair, 1 per table, 12 available.
  2. Z = 3x1 + 2x2.
  3. 3, one per ≤ constraint: x1 + x2 + x3 = 8, 2x1 + x2 + x4 = 12, x2 + x5 = 6.

This workshop program is used again in A2, A3, B1, C2, C3, D1 and D2.

A2Reading slacks at a pointWarm-up

Workshop program: x1 + x2 + x3 = 8, 2x1 + x2 + x4 = 12, x2 + x5 = 6. Make 3 chairs and 4 tables.

Find the three slacks and the profit.

Is (3, 4) feasible?

Show solution
  • x3 = 8 − 3 − 4 = 1 hour of labour left.
  • x4 = 12 − 6 − 4 = 2 machine hours left.
  • x5 = 6 − 4 = 2 more tables could be sold.
  • Z = 3·3 + 2·4 = 17. All slacks ≥ 0, so the point is feasible (but not a corner: no variable is 0).
A3Corners are basic solutionsStandard

At a corner, the variables equal to zero are out of the basis.

1. Which two variables are zero at A(6, 0)?

2. And at C(2, 6)?

3. Basic variables at C: their values.

Show solution
  1. At A: x2 = 0 and the machine is full, so x4 = 12 − 12 = 0. x2, x4.
  2. At C: labour 2 + 6 = 8 is full (x3 = 0) and tables 6 = 6 are at the cap (x5 = 0). x3, x5.
  3. x4 = 12 − 2·2 − 6 = 2; Z = 3·2 + 2·6 = 18.

B3Breaking a tieStandard

Maximise Z = 3x1 + 3x2 with 4x1 + x2 ≤ 12 and x1 + 2x2 ≤ 10. The Z row has a tie: 3 and 3.

VDBx1x2x3x4cste
x3411012
x4120110
Z33000

1. If x1 enters, its smallest ratio and the new Z. 2. Same if x2 enters.

3. Which one should enter?

Show solution
  • x1: ratios 12 ÷ 4 = 3, 10 ÷ 1 = 10 → it can rise to 3, Z = 3 × 3 = 9.
  • x2: ratios 12 ÷ 1 = 12, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 → it can rise to 5, Z = 3 × 5 = 15.
  • Equal coefficients, so take the larger ratio: x2 enters (x4 leaves, pivot 2). Either way the optimum is (2, 4), Z = 18, here in 2 pivots both ways.

E2A full run on your ownChallenge

Maximise Z = 4x1 + 5x2 subject to x1 + 2x2 ≤ 10, 3x1 + 2x2 ≤ 18, x1 ≤ 5, all ≥ 0.

Rules: enter = largest positive coefficient in the Z row · ratio = cste ÷ entering-column coefficient, only where that coefficient is > 0 · leave = smallest ratio · cste of the Z row = −Z.

1. Path of the simplex: first vertex reached and its Z, then the optimum.

2. Slacks at the optimum?

Show solution
  1. Z row 4, 5 → x2 enters; ratios 10 ÷ 2 = 5, 18 ÷ 2 = 9, +∞ → x3 leaves, pivot 2. Vertex (0, 5), Z = 25.
  2. Z = 25 + 3/2·x1 − 5/2·x3 → x1 enters; ratios 5 ÷ 1/2 = 10, 8 ÷ 2 = 4, 5 ÷ 1 = 5 → x4 leaves, pivot 2. Vertex (4, 3), Z = 31.
  3. Z = 31 − 7/4·x3 − 3/4·x4: optimal. Slacks: x3 = 10 − 4 − 6 = 0, x4 = 18 − 12 − 6 = 0, x5 = 5 − 4 = 1.
