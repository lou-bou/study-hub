Project or not? (projet ou non-projet)

↑ contents

Lesson sections 1–3. Auto-checked.

A1Project or not?Warm-up

Decide for each mission whether it is a project.

Checklist: unique · some novelty · a sponsor · a project manager · a defined goal · a defined deadline · a defined budget. Red flags: routine · no end date · tiny amount.
1. Within 6 months, move the company's email to a cloud service, budget 2 M DA
2. Back up the servers every night
3. Improve customer satisfaction
4. By June, open a second shop in Oran, ordered by the CEO
5. Answer customer emails every day
6. Replace one broken mouse in the accounting office
7. Before next semester, build an app for USTHB students to book study rooms
8. Keep the website online
Show solution
  1. Within 6 months, move the company's email to a cloud service, budget 2 M DA: project (one-off, dated, budgeted)
  2. Back up the servers every night: not a project (routine)
  3. Improve customer satisfaction: not a project (vague, no deadline)
  4. By June, open a second shop in Oran, ordered by the CEO: project (unique, sponsor, deadline)
  5. Answer customer emails every day: not a project (routine)
  6. Replace one broken mouse in the accounting office: not a project (amount far too small)
  7. Before next semester, build an app for USTHB students to book study rooms: project (new product, deadline)
  8. Keep the website online: not a project (routine, no end)
A2Which red flag?Warm-up

Each mission below is not a project. Name the main reason.

1. Check the stock levels every Monday
2. Become the best company in the sector
3. Redesign the logo "whenever we have time"
4. Buy a new stapler for reception
5. Pay suppliers at the end of each month
Show solution
  1. Check the stock levels every Monday: routine task
  2. Become the best company in the sector: goal too vague (no measurable goal)
  3. Redesign the logo "whenever we have time": no end date
  4. Buy a new stapler for reception: amount too small
  5. Pay suppliers at the end of each month: routine task
A3Which condition is missing?Standard

1. Everyone in the company agrees a new intranet is needed. IT has a team, a budget of 3 M DA and a deadline in March. Nobody in management has formally ordered it. Which condition is missing? one answer

2. A director asks: "Make our sales grow." He names Sara as manager and gives her 1 M DA. What must Sara do first? one answer

3. Which list gives the five elements needed, ideally, to start a project? one answer

Show solution

1. Correct: b). This is the course's "trap": everyone agrees the project is needed, but there is no sponsor to answer for it.

2. Correct: c). When the goal is missing, defining it with the sponsor is the manager's first task, e.g. "+10% sales in the East region by December".

3. Correct: a). Client, sponsor, project manager, a problem or need, and a goal.

Who is who (les acteurs)

B1Sponsor, manager, client or funder?Warm-up

Who does it?

Sponsor (mandant): orders the project and answers for results, cost and deadline. Project manager (chef de projet): runs it day to day. Client: pays and benefits. Funder (sponsor in French): outside party paying for part.
1. Names the project manager and the steering committee
2. Assigns tasks to team members and follows the weekly schedule
3. A telecom company pays part of a student hackathon in exchange for its logo on the posters
4. Answers to top management for reaching the project's goals within cost and deadline
5. Is the link between the client and the team during the whole project
6. Will pay for the finished product and use it
Show solution
  1. Names the project manager and the steering committee: sponsor (mandant)
  2. Assigns tasks to team members and follows the weekly schedule: project manager
  3. A telecom company pays part of a student hackathon in exchange for its logo on the posters: funder
  4. Answers to top management for reaching the project's goals within cost and deadline: sponsor (mandant)
  5. Is the link between the client and the team during the whole project: project manager
  6. Will pay for the finished product and use it: client
B2MOA or MOE?Warm-up

Client side (maître d'ouvrage) or supplier side (maître d'œuvre)?

1. The town hall that orders a new sports hall
2. The building firm that constructs the sports hall
3. Checks that the work conforms to the needs expressed
4. Is chosen for its technical skill to execute the work within time, cost and quality
5. Carries out financial control of the project and attends project reviews
6. The IT services company that develops a bank's mobile app
Show solution
  1. The town hall that orders a new sports hall: MOA · project owner (client)
  2. The building firm that constructs the sports hall: MOE · prime contractor (executes the work)
  3. Checks that the work conforms to the needs expressed: MOA · project owner
  4. Is chosen for its technical skill to execute the work within time, cost and quality: MOE · prime contractor
  5. Carries out financial control of the project and attends project reviews: MOA · project owner
  6. The IT services company that develops a bank's mobile app: MOE · prime contractor
B3Which Big Data job?Standard

Match each task to the role that does it.

Collect & store: Data Engineer · CDO · Data Miner · Big Data Architect. Process & analyse: Data Analyst · Data Scientist · Data Manager · ML Engineer. Protect: DPO · Data Steward.
1. Designs the system that collects sensor data every minute and prepares it for analysts
2. Turns sales figures into charts that managers understand
3. Builds a model predicting which customers will leave next month
4. Develops a deep neural network that recognises products in photos
5. Makes sure stored personal data is safe from intrusions and complies with GDPR
6. Leads the data team and gives top management access to results
7. Chooses Hadoop, Spark and Cassandra and inventories the available data
8. Decides who may access which database and checks that sources are reliable
Show solution
  1. Designs the system that collects sensor data every minute and prepares it for analysts: Data Engineer
  2. Turns sales figures into charts that managers understand: Data Analyst ("makes the data talk")
  3. Builds a model predicting which customers will leave next month: Data Scientist (predictive models)
  4. Develops a deep neural network that recognises products in photos: ML Engineer
  5. Makes sure stored personal data is safe from intrusions and complies with GDPR: DPO (security + legal)
  6. Leads the data team and gives top management access to results: Chief Data Officer
  7. Chooses Hadoop, Spark and Cassandra and inventories the available data: Big Data Architect
  8. Decides who may access which database and checks that sources are reliable: Data Steward

Organising the project (organisation, avant-projet)

↑ contents

Lesson sections 5–7. Auto-checked.

C1Which organisation?Warm-up

Choose the structure that fits each case.

Matrix: members from several departments, part time, two bosses. Project organisation: manager is the hierarchical boss, long and large projects. Staff (état-major): no real team, a few coordination meetings.
1. A 4-month campaign needing 1 person from marketing, 1 from IT and 1 from sales, each part time
2. Building a new airport terminal: 40 engineers full time for 5 years
3. Choosing a new photocopier supplier: a few meetings with users, then a decision
4. A bank's "HotLine 7/7": 9 people from 6 departments plus the manager
Show solution
  1. A 4-month campaign needing 1 person from marketing, 1 from IT and 1 from sales, each part time: matrix
  2. Building a new airport terminal: 40 engineers full time for 5 years: project organisation (long, large)
  3. Choosing a new photocopier supplier: a few meetings with users, then a decision: staff (état-major) (no team needed)
  4. A bank's "HotLine 7/7": 9 people from 6 departments plus the manager: matrix (course example)
C2Full-time equivalents and the half-day ruleStandard

A matrix project needs 1 full-time equivalent (FTE). Option X: 4 people at 25%. Option Y: 2 people at 50%.

FTE = number of people × share of time. Half-day rule: the manager spends about ½ day per week per team member on organising.
Show solution
  1. X FTE: 4 × 0.25 = 1 FTE (same as Y: 2 × 0.5 = 1).
  2. X lead: 4 × ½ = 2 days a week of organising.
  3. Y lead: 2 × ½ = 1 day a week. Same capacity, half the overhead: prefer Y.
  4. 6 people: a team of 6 costs the manager 6 × ½ = 3 days a week.
C3Advantage or drawback?Standard

Each line is an advantage or a drawback of one structure. Which?

1. Double hierarchy: each member has two bosses
2. Project managers have high autonomy
3. Little communication between projects
4. Workload can be balanced across departments
5. The company's life is tied to how long projects last
6. Members are absorbed by daily, urgent work and neglect the project
Show solution
  1. Double hierarchy: each member has two bosses: matrix · drawback
  2. Project managers have high autonomy: project org · advantage
  3. Little communication between projects: project org · drawback
  4. Workload can be balanced across departments: matrix · advantage (gestion des charges)
  5. The company's life is tied to how long projects last: project org · drawback
  6. Members are absorbed by daily, urgent work and neglect the project: matrix · drawback (from the "pièges")
C4Pre-project goal or project goal?Standard

A hospital wants a new patient-records system. Sort each goal. The course warns: do not confuse the two.

1. Compare the 4 software packages on the market
2. Replace the paper files with one digital system used by all wards
3. Estimate costs and benefits to ±20%
4. Train all 300 nurses on the new system
5. Check what the data-protection law requires
Show solution
  1. Compare the 4 software packages on the market: pre-project goal (a study)
  2. Replace the paper files with one digital system used by all wards: project goal (tangible result)
  3. Estimate costs and benefits to ±20%: pre-project goal
  4. Train all 300 nurses on the new system: project goal
  5. Check what the data-protection law requires: pre-project goal (analysis)

D1Which phase?Warm-up

Place each output in its phase of the management process.

Initiation: charter, scope, deliverables, go/no-go. Planning: schedule, resources, risks, communication. Execution: results, reports, team. Control: deadlines, changes. Closing: acceptance, supplier closure, review (bilan).
1. The project charter (charte)
2. A list of risks with how to handle each
3. A weekly progress report
4. A client's change request, analysed and accepted with a new date
5. Lessons learned: strengths and weaknesses for the next project
6. The decision whether the project will be done at all
7. The resource plan: who works when
8. Final sign-off by the client
Show solution
  1. The project charter (charte): Initiation
  2. A list of risks with how to handle each: Planning
  3. A weekly progress report: Execution
  4. A client's change request, analysed and accepted with a new date: Monitoring & control
  5. Lessons learned: strengths and weaknesses for the next project: Closing
  6. The decision whether the project will be done at all: Initiation
  7. The resource plan: who works when: Planning
  8. Final sign-off by the client: Closing
D2Which side of the triangle breaks?Warm-up

For each event, which PCD criterion is not respected?

P performance (technical quality) · C cost (economic) · D deadline (time).
1. The app is delivered on time and on budget but crashes when 100 users log in
2. Everything works, on time, but 30% over budget
3. The building is perfect and within budget, but opens 3 months late
4. The client asked for an extra feature that was added for free, and the team paid for it
Show solution
  1. The app is delivered on time and on budget but crashes when 100 users log in: P · performance
  2. Everything works, on time, but 30% over budget: C · cost
  3. The building is perfect and within budget, but opens 3 months late: D · deadline
  4. The client asked for an extra feature that was added for free, and the team paid for it: C · cost (should have been renegotiated)
D3Non-qualityStandard

1. A serious technical problem is found late. Extra engineers are added. What happens? one answer

2. The same problem is found early. What happens? one answer

3. Which statement matches the course? one answer

Show solution

1. Correct: b). Found late, even extra resources cannot save the date: schedule and cost drift.

2. Correct: a). Extra resources keep P and D but blow the budget.

3. Correct: c). Both non-quality and over-quality cost money; acceptable quality lies between.

D4Milestone or not?Standard

A milestone (jalon) is a significant event with a certain date, reached when its result is irreversibly acquired.

1. The sponsor accepts the project management plan
2. The team's Tuesday coffee break
3. Delivery of the new servers
4. "We are about halfway through coding, more or less"
5. Final acceptance (recette) by the client
Show solution
  1. The sponsor accepts the project management plan: milestone
  2. The team's Tuesday coffee break: not a milestone
  3. Delivery of the new servers: milestone (réception d'un équipement)
  4. "We are about halfway through coding, more or less": not a milestone (not certain or irreversible)
  5. Final acceptance (recette) by the client: milestone

E1Finish the 5 whysCase

Students ask the faculty for "more computers in the library". Before buying any, you ask why. Write a plausible chain of whys and the real problem you might find.

Method: ask "why?" up to five times, each time to whoever can answer. Stop when you reach a cause you can act on. The stated problem is often the false one.

Show solution

One possible chain (many answers are fine):

  1. Why more computers? Students queue for an hour.
  2. Why do they queue? Every PC is busy all afternoon.
  3. Why all afternoon? Students use them to print their reports.
  4. Why print here? The only printer on campus is connected only to library PCs.
  5. Why? The network printer was never set up for personal laptops.

Real problem: printing access, not number of computers. Fix: open the printer to laptops. Buying PCs would solve the false problem.

E2How many sprints?Case

A Big Data team estimates its product backlog at 90 story points. Its velocity is 15 points per sprint, and sprints last 3 weeks.

Sprints = backlog ÷ velocity. Duration = sprints × sprint length.
Show solution
  1. Sprints: 90 ÷ 15 = 6.
  2. Duration: 6 × 3 = 18 weeks, with a deliverable to the client at the end of every sprint.
E3Pre-project estimate to ±20%Case

A pre-project estimates the project cost at 300 000 DA, with a precision of ±20%. Give the range.

Show solution

20% of 300 000 = 60 000. Range: 300 000 − 60 000 = 240 000 DA to 300 000 + 60 000 = 360 000 DA.

E4Choose the project managerCase

A clinic wants a Big Data project to predict appointment no-shows. Candidates: Amine, brilliant ML engineer, never led a team; Nadia, head of the appointments office, led the clinic's last software roll-out, communicates well, basic data knowledge; Karim, external consultant, strong in management, knows nothing about clinics. Following the course, who is the best choice? one answer

Show solution

Correct: b). Nadia has led a similar project, communicates well and knows the domain. Like Meyer at FORMALUX, a coach can fill her data gap. Pure technical skill (Amine) or pure management without domain knowledge (Karim) are the course's two traps.

E5Turn a vague wish into a projectCase

The dean says: "We should use our data better." Rewrite this as a project: give a goal with a measurable result and a deadline, name a plausible sponsor and project manager, and say which Big Data roles you would need.

Check: unique · novelty · sponsor · project manager · defined goal · deadline · budget.

Show solution

Example answer:

  • Goal: by the end of the academic year, a dashboard showing, per module, pass rates and attendance, updated weekly, to cut the failure rate in L1 by 10%.
  • Sponsor: the dean. Project manager: the vice-dean for teaching, who knows the faculty and has led the e-learning roll-out.
  • Budget: fixed by the dean, e.g. for servers and 2 part-time staff.
  • Roles: Data Engineer (collect grades and attendance), Data Analyst (dashboard), Data Steward or DPO (student data is personal: access and GDPR-style rules).
